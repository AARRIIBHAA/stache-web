Questions worth asking.

Does Stache connect to my bank account?

No, and it never will ask you for banking logins. Stache has no access to your accounts, no screen-scraping, and no third-party aggregator sitting between you and your bank. You add expenses yourself — by photographing a receipt or typing an amount. That is the whole reason it works the same across Capitec, FNB, Standard Bank, TymeBank, Discovery and the cash in your wallet.

Where is my data stored?

On servers in the European Union, encrypted in transit and at rest. Your expenses are tied to your account and are not sold, shared or used to build an advertising profile of you.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet — Stache is iOS only for now. Building one app properly beats shipping two badly, and Android is the obvious next step rather than a maybe.

How do I cancel Premium?

Through your Apple ID subscription settings, any time, in a few taps. There is no retention flow to argue with and no phone call to make. You keep Premium until the end of the period you've already paid for, then drop back to the free plan with all your data intact.

What happens if I delete my account?

Everything goes — expenses, receipts, budgets and profile — and it is permanent. There is no recovery window and no archived copy sitting on a backup somewhere waiting to be restored. If there is anything you want to keep, export a statement before you delete.

Built by one person, in South Africa, for the way we actually bank.

Stache started with a shoebox. Slips from Checkers and Engen and the pharmacy, a card that was somehow always emptier than it should have been, and no honest answer to a simple question: where did the month go?

The budgeting apps on offer were built somewhere else, for someone else. They wanted to link to a bank they'd never heard of. They wanted dollars. They assumed one account, one card, one tidy financial life — not the four accounts and the cash in the cubbyhole that most South Africans are actually working with.

So this one is built the other way around. No bank logins. No pretending cash doesn't exist. Rands, till slips, and one clean ledger that adds up at the end of the month. It's a solo project, which means it moves at the pace of one person's evenings and weekends — but it also means there's nobody to overrule the decision to keep it simple.

One clean ledger for every rand you spend.
